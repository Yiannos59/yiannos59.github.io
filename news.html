Humble Earths

Beyond the Western Frame — Transcultural Modernisms


Limassol Municipal Art Gallery

311, 28th October Street, Limassol 3105, Cyprus



Initiated by the In Situ group, Humble Earths: Beyond the Western Frame — Transcultural Modernisms presents an expanded perspective on modernism, exploring environmental concerns, intercultural dialogue and artistic practices that extend beyond a Western-centred narrative.


Following exhibitions held in 2024 and 2025 at the Paphos Municipal Arts Centre, Larnaca Municipal Gallery and Zincirli Mosque in Serres, Greece, the exhibition comes to the Limassol Municipal Art Gallery, bringing together artists from Cyprus, Bulgaria and the United Kingdom. A reciprocal exhibition is planned for Sofia, Bulgaria, in April 2027.


Contemporary works are presented in dialogue with those of Christoforos Savva, Stass Paraskos and Glyn Hughes, three significant figures whose practices contribute to a broader understanding of modernism in Cyprus. Drawn from the Limassol Municipal Collection, their works provide a point of departure for reimagining the relationship between modernist lineage and contemporary artistic practice.


The exhibition explores how these three pioneering artists engaged with international artistic movements without uncritically adopting the dominant conventions of Western modernism. Rather than following established artistic centres, they developed distinctive visual languages shaped by their cultural contexts and by their individual and shared artistic concerns.


Bringing modernist works into dialogue with contemporary artistic practices, Humble Earths examines the continuing influence of this artistic legacy while considering how changing social, political and environmental conditions have shaped new cultural concerns. The division of Cyprus in 1974, the escalating ecological crisis and the impact of the global economy on everyday life have all introduced new questions and perspectives into contemporary artistic practice.


Through this transcultural dialogue across generations, the exhibition invites the public to reimagine modernism not as a singular movement defined primarily through a Western perspective, but as a plurality of artistic practices shaped by diverse histories, places and experiences.
